# Creative Brief — Forecourt

## Core promise
Alone on the 3am shift at an isolated services, a cashier is confronted through the night glass by a
man with blood on him who needs something she can give — and every reason not to give it. Over
twenty minutes she makes one small decision she cannot take back, for reasons that are not clean,
and is left not knowing whether she helped a victim or shielded someone dangerous.

## Desired reader experience
The pressure of choosing under real fear and real doubt; the cost of acting when you cannot know;
recognition without relief.

## Theme question
When someone dangerous needs your help, what do you owe them, and what do you owe yourself? (Left
open — enacted, never answered.)

## Genre obligations
Grounded literary realism with genuine tension. A single transforming consciousness; a threat that
cannot be resolved by proof; the mechanics of a night petrol kiosk (the glass, the pay drawer, the
alarm) doing the dramatic work; a taut, single-sitting, single-location shape.

## Deliberate deviations
No twist, no reveal: the man's true nature is never confirmed. No rescue and no violence climax. The
turn is a small concrete act from behind the glass, and its consequences stay uncertain.

## Content boundaries
Blood is present; no on-page violence. The man is neither villain nor saint. Jo's fear and her
son-shaped recognition are real but never named as such. No sentimental uplift; the debt to a
stranger is not settled.

## Originality risks
(1) The two-hander tempts the theme into dialogue (the probe's flagged risk) — hold it in the glass,
the drawer, and one thing Jo does; nobody says what it means. (2) The night-shift-threat setup
invites thriller cliche (the twist, the reveal, the gun) — refuse all three; the tension is moral,
not plotted. (3) Jo's choice invites saintly compassion — keep her reason tangled (fear + exhaustion
+ the son she can't reach), never noble.

## Open questions
Does the man stay genuinely ambiguous, and does Jo's choice read as un-saintly rather than kind?
Route to the adversarial reader and the character simulator.
