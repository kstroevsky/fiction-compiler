The phone was in her hand before she chose to pick it up. Esther's name sat at the top of the list, and under her thumb the screen threw a hard blue light across the flour on the bench. Three streets over the ward would be dark. A nurse would go down the row and wake her, and Esther would surface out of whatever the fall and the drugs had left her, and she would say, in the voice she kept for a scaled measure read back wrong, tell me exactly what you did.

Nadia stood with the light in her hand and the ruined tub in the sink and the twelve tins waiting.

She set the phone face down on the bench.

There was a milk bread she could make in the hours she had. Not Esther's. Her own, or near enough — a straight dough, more yeast, a warm fast rise by the oven mouth instead of the long cold one. It would come out plainer. Tighter crumb, less of that pull Esther's loaf gave when you tore it. But it would be bread, and it would be twelve, and it would stand at the counter by six.

She scalded fresh milk, and this time she stood over the pan. The first scald she had ever done she rushed, weeks ago, learning; she took it past the skin and Esther made her pour the pan down the drain and start again. Milk's dear, Esther said. Waste is a decision. Tonight she caught it at the shiver before the boil and pulled it, and let it fall to blood heat while she scaled the flour.

Her hands ran the measures without her having to send them. That was the strange part, and she let it be strange. Flour, salt, the sugar that would feed the fast yeast, the yeast itself bloomed in a spoon of the warm milk until it foamed at the rim. She made the well and poured and drew it together with the flat of her hand, and the dough came up under her the way dough came, a live weight that leaned back against the push.

She turned it onto the bench and put the heels of her palms into it. Push, fold, quarter turn. Push, fold, quarter turn. The wood took the rhythm up through her arms and shoulders. Somewhere in the middle of it the arithmetic stopped running. There was the dough going from ragged to smooth, from slack to a skin that sprang back at a fingertip, coming alive the way the other batch had stopped being alive.

She oiled a bowl, set the dough by the oven, and lit the oven low to throw its heat into the corner. Ten to three by the clock over the door. A fast dough would rise warm in an hour, and she had the hour. Knock it back, shape it, a short second prove in the tins, and it would go into the heat with time to spare before six.

The phone lay face down where she had left it. She did not turn it over.
