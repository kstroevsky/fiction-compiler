The phone was in her hand before she decided to reach for it. Esther's name at the top of the list. Under her thumb the screen lit the flour blue.

They would wake her. A nurse down the dark row, a hand on the shoulder, and Esther coming up out of the fall to say, in the measure-read-back-wrong voice, tell me exactly what you did.

Nadia set the phone face down.

She could make a milk bread in the hours left. Not Esther's — a straight dough, more yeast, a warm rise by the oven instead of the long cold one. Plainer. Tighter in the crumb. But twelve of them, at the counter, by six.

Fresh milk in the pan, and this time she stood over it. The first scald she ever did she had rushed and taken past the skin, and Esther had made her pour it out. Milk's dear. Waste is a decision. She caught it now at the shiver before the boil and pulled it to cool.

Her hands ran the measures ahead of her. Flour, salt, sugar for the fast yeast, the yeast foaming in a spoon of warm milk. She made the well and brought it together with the flat of her hand, and the dough leaned back against her palm, a live weight.

Onto the bench. Push, fold, turn. Push, fold, turn. The arithmetic quit somewhere in the rhythm. There was only the dough coming smooth under her hands, a skin that sprang back, alive where the other had died.

She set it by the oven to rise and lit the oven low. Ten to three. An hour warm and it would be ready to shape, and then the tins, and then the heat, with time to cool before six.

The phone lay where she had put it. She left it there.
