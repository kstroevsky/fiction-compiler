Two in the morning. Nadia had the shop to herself.

The ovens were cold. Twelve tins stood greased and lined on the bench — the Halloran order, milk loaves for the wake, due at six. Esther had written it in the book weeks ago, before the stepladder, before the ward three streets away. Tonight the overnight was Nadia's.

She had done it by the book. Flour scaled twice. Milk scalded, cooled to blood heat on her wrist. The dough into the walk-in at eight to proof slow and cold, the way the milk loaf wanted. Then the leaving alone, which was the whole trick of it.

Time to shape.

The walk-in was cold on her arms. She lifted the cloth off the tub, and the smell reached her before her eyes did — sharp, sour under, yeast gone too far. The dough had risen over the rim and dropped back on itself. She pressed a thumb to it. The dent stayed.

Over-proofed. Dead.

The proofer's dial read high. The thermostat had drifted the way it always drifted, the way Esther walked it down three times a shift with a rag over the vent. Nadia had checked it at ten and not again.

Four hours to six. Esther's dough took eight. The numbers did not move for being stared at.

She carried the tub to the sink and ran water into it until the smell went down the drain. The tins waited in their row for bread that was not coming out of that cold.
