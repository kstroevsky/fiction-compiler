# Creative Brief — Salt in the Wire

## Core promise
A signal-station operator discovers the automated distress relay was not broken by weather but
cut by hand — years before her posting — and that she has been unknowingly keeping a silence
someone chose. The story is her finding out why, and deciding what to do with an inheritance
she never agreed to.

## Desired reader experience
Quiet dread, then complicity. The reader should reach the end unable to say cleanly whether
Mara (or her predecessor) did right — and should feel that the ambiguity was earned, not dodged.

## Theme question
When following the rule would kill and breaking it saved lives, is inherited silence a betrayal
or a mercy? The story must *stage* this, not argue it.

## Genre obligations
Literary/quiet-speculative. Restraint, concrete procedural detail, one focalizer, moral weight.
No supernatural mechanism; the uncanny is ethical.

## Deliberate deviations
- The "sabotage" is an act of mercy, not malice — subverts the tampering-thriller default.
- No outside antagonist; the opposing force is duty/protocol itself.
- The predecessor is not a villain to expose but a conscience to inherit.

## Content boundaries
Implied past deaths (a wreck), no on-page gore. No romance subplot.

## Originality risks (defaults to resist)
- Storm/outsider as the cause (forbidden).
- A tidy confession scene that resolves the moral question.
- Mara narrating her own feelings ("she felt torn").
- A hopeful, duty-affirming ending that erases the cost.

## Open questions (for the human gate)
- Does Mara restore the relay, preserve the silence, or find a third act? (Ending gate.)
- Is Jonas alive to confront, or present only through logs? (Currently: alive, ashore.)
