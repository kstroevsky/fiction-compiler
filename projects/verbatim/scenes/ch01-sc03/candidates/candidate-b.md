The decisive question came in the last hour, when the light through the high windows had gone the color of the water jug.

Counsel wanted it plain for the record. On the night the raiders came to the sluice, did the witness see the accused — and here the name, and the panel leaning the little that panels lean — lead them, or be led? Say what you saw.

Josif took his time; he was good at time. He set his hands on the rail the way he had set nothing else down all day, looked not at the panel and not at Mira but at the middle distance where the valley was, and gave her the word.

He gave it with both hands and one meaning. All through his testimony he had been walking the court toward the lamp — the path, the sluice, the man who goes ahead in the dark — so that by the time the word came it could carry only the one sense, *he led them*, and a faithful tongue would carry that and nothing after it. He had built her a word with a single hand and set it in her mouth.

Mira held the stem of the microphone. In the good ear the word waited, and it had two hands after all; she was the only one left in the room who still held the other.

"The witness uses a word," she said, in the flat register, to the panel, to the record, to the stenographer whose keys had never once been wrong on her account, "that our languages do not have. It means a man who goes ahead in the dark. It does not say whether he goes to lead them, or because they drove him out in front. In his tongue it is one word. I cannot make it two for you. You will have to."

The room took a moment to understand that the interpreter had spoken, and then that she had stopped. The stenographer's hands hung over the keys, waiting for a rendering that did not come, and set down what she had said instead.

She drank the water at her elbow; she had not touched it all day. Across the chamber the old man turned his face back to the middle distance, to the valley, to the lamp he had carried down all this way and could set now in no hand but his own. The panel leaned together to confer. In the next hour they would weigh a word she had refused to halve, and a man would go out into the morning or he would not, and the record would carry her words where the word should have stood — in her own voice, which the record had never before had reason to name.
