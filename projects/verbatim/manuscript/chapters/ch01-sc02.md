They could not speak. That was the first rule they had taught her and the one the room believed in most: a witness and his voice kept apart in the recess, the way you keep a flame from what it would burn. So when they brought Josif down the back corridor between two officers, and the corridor was narrow, and Mira had flattened herself against the cold wall to let them pass, neither of them said a word the record could have wanted.

He was smaller out of the box. Up close the valley was all over him — the hands, the way the collar sat. He did not look at her. He looked at the water cooler humming against the wall, the paper cones beside it, and he did not take one, and to the officer who offered he said, in the hill way, that a man does not drink where he is going. The officer heard an old man refuse a cup. Mira had the rest of it, the half that lived in the breath: where he was going was nowhere he expected to be given water again.

Then, still to the cooler, still to no one, he said the thing about the lamp.

In the valley they kept a saying for the one who goes ahead in the dark so the others can follow, and it was a kind saying, a saying for shepherds and midwives and the first boat out after a storm. Josif turned it over the way he had turned *vedel*, and gave it its other face — the one who goes ahead with the lamp so the dark has something to follow him to. He said it of no one. He said it to the water cooler, in a dialect the corridor did not keep, and then the officers had him past her and round the corner, and the saying stayed behind in the cold like a coat someone had dropped.

Radan had carried the lamp. Not been led — led. The one witness who could have said so was dead, and the law would weigh what it could hold in its hands and find it light, and let him out into a morning; and Josif knew all of this the way she knew it now, and had known it longer, and had come down out of the valley to set it in the one pair of hands the law could not watch.

Hers.

She stood in the corridor after they had gone and did not go back in. Down the hall a door opened on the sound of the chamber, the flat voices, the shuffle of the panel returning, and closed again. She would have to go in. She had a word waiting for her in there, and now it had only one hand.
