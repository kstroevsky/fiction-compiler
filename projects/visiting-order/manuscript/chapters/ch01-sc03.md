The officer at the desk stood, and that was time. All down the hall the visits ended at once, the chairs going back, the last things said fast because there was no more room for them.

"Right," Danny said. He stood. "You'll not leave it a month."

"I'll come when I come."

"You always do."

They had the one hold at the end, the same as the start. His chin came down on the top of her head for a second, from when she was small and he wasn't, before any of it. "Tell Mam," he said, into her hair, and then the officer was at his elbow, and he let go and grinned. "Tell her I said." He didn't say what.

"I will," Lena said.

She went out the way she'd come, the double doors a set at a time, the locker, her phone lighting up with nothing that mattered, the stamp still on the back of her hand where you couldn't see it without the lamp. The car was cold. She sat in it a minute before the key.

She took the photo out of her back pocket. Mam at the window, the light through her hair, her eyes gone past the camera and past the room. It was a good photo. It had come out well. Danny would have held it under the strip light the whole visit and asked to keep it, and it would have shown him nothing he wanted: his mother's face, and him nowhere in it.

Lena put it back in her pocket. She'd bring one next month. A worse one, a kinder one, one from a better day if there was a better day, and if there wasn't she'd say the camera on the phone was playing up. She'd think of something. She always did.

She turned the key. Three hours back if the roads were with her, and they wouldn't be.
