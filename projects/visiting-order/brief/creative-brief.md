# Creative Brief — Visiting Order

## Core promise
A woman visits her brother in prison carrying one sentence she could say and shouldn't: their
mother has stopped asking after him. Over a single visiting hour she decides, on camera, whether to
put that weight down or keep carrying it — and keeps it, for reasons that are not clean.

## Desired reader experience
The weight of a kindness that costs the giver; the loneliness of being the one who shows up;
recognition without relief.

## Theme question
Is a kind lie a betrayal, or the last way left to keep faith with someone? (Left open — enacted,
never answered.)

## Genre obligations
Grounded literary realism. A single transforming consciousness; an interpersonal conflict that no
proof can settle; the mechanics of a prison visit doing the dramatic work; a legible, single-sitting
shape.

## Deliberate deviations
Refusal, not resolution. No confession, no catharsis, no reconciliation. The mother is not restored;
the old imbalance (Mam loved Danny best) is not healed; Danny never learns the truth.

## Content boundaries
No on-page violence; the crime is left almost entirely unstated; grief and resentment are present
but never named as such; no sentimental uplift.

## Originality risks
(1) The two-hander tempts the theme into dialogue — the fault flagged by the premise probe. Mitigation:
the theme is carried by one withheld sentence and by the repeated concrete line "Mam sends her love";
no character or narrator states it. (2) The prison visit trades easily in cliche (the bolted table,
the tears). Mitigation: keep the register dry, the detail specific and procedural, the emotion in the
hands and the rules.

## Open questions
Does Lena's choice read as complicated (pity + resentment + exhaustion + love) rather than saintly?
Route to the character simulator and the adversarial reader.
