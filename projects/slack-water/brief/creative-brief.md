# Creative Brief — Slack Water

## Core promise
A working shipwright is asked to launch a dead man's boat on the morning tide. Fresh paint
hides structural rot. In one continuous morning she must tell his grieving son the truth about
the wood — and find the one honest way to give him the launching anyway.

## Desired reader experience
The steadiness of competence telling an unwelcome truth; grief given an honest shape rather
than a consolation; quiet recognition at the close, not triumph.

## Theme question
When is it truer to let a made thing be finished than to force it to work?

## Genre obligations
Grounded literary realism. A concrete, physically-caused problem; a protagonist whose judgment
(not luck) turns it; sensory, plainspoken prose; a legible, single-sitting shape.

## Deliberate deviations
The "expert saves the day" beat is refused: Rennie cannot repair the boat and does not. The
resolution is an honest compromise (a launching that is not a launch), not a rescue.

## Content boundaries
No on-page death; grief is present but never named as such; no sentimental uplift; the customer
does not deliver tidy thanks; the yard's money troubles are not resolved.

## Originality risks
The "wise craftsperson teaches the grieving customer a life lesson" cliche. Mitigation: the turn
is enacted (knife, hose, tide, haul-out), never spoken as a moral; Aldous fights every true thing;
the ending lands on a concrete image, not a feeling.

## Open questions
Does the honest launching read as earned rather than engineered? (Route to the adversarial reader.)
