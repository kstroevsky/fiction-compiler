# Dialogue and Subtext

**Concept:** dialogue-subtext · **Layer:** craft
**Used by:** `style-editor` agent, `character-simulator` agent

## Definition
Dialogue is *verbal action*: each speaker pursues an objective using talk as a tactic. The
meaning lives in the gap between what is said (text) and what is wanted or meant (subtext).
Characters rarely state their real aim; they angle, deflect, test, concede, and attack. A line
should do something — shift status, extract information, wound, protect — not just transmit it.

## Use when
Writing or auditing any exchange. Give each participant an objective and something they cannot
say directly; let tactics change when they fail.

## Diagnostic questions
- What does each speaker want from the other in this exchange?
- What can't be said aloud, and how does it leak (evasion, over-precision, silence)?
- Does the relationship stand differently at the end of the exchange than the start?
- Could I swap the name tags and not notice? (Idiolect failure.)

## Failure modes
- On-the-nose dialogue: characters say exactly what they feel and mean.
- Exposition delivery: talk exists to inform the reader, not to act on each other.
- Uniform voice: everyone shares the model's articulate register.
- Adverb crutches in tags ("she said angrily") doing the work the line should do.

## Conflicts with
Clarity — subtext can obscure. Calibrate to the reader contract's accessibility.
