# Character Intentionality

**Concept:** character-intentionality · **Layer:** craft
**Used by:** `character-simulator` agent, `constitution/quality-contract.md`

## Definition
Characters must act *from* interpretable interiors — desire, need, values, fear, belief
(including false belief), knowledge, and relationship state — not to satisfy the author's
outline. Believability is a chain of legible intention, even when the character is wrong,
irrational, or self-deceiving. Irrationality is fine; *unmotivated* action is not.

## Use when
Proposing any consequential action or line. Simulate the character forward: given what they
believe and want *right now*, and what they know, what would they actually try — and what
would they misjudge?

## Diagnostic questions
- What does this character believe, want, risk, notice, and misunderstand here?
- Would they do this if the plot did not need it? If not, whose need is it really?
- Where is the cost? A choice without cost reveals little.
- What is the strongest objection the character themself would raise?

## Failure modes
- Plot-puppet action (moves that serve the outline, not the person).
- Therapeutic modernity: characters with anachronistic, tidy self-awareness.
- Flat competence: no blind spots, no self-deception, no contradiction.
- Everyone equally articulate about their own feelings.

## Conflicts with
Pace — full motivation can bloat. Compress via implication, not omission.
