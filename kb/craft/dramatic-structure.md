# Dramatic Structure

**Concept:** dramatic-structure · **Layer:** craft
**Used by:** `narrative-architect` agent, `planning/event-graph.json`

## Definition
The shape of a complete action. The classical account (Aristotle) is a single *unified* action
with a *complication* (tying) and an *unraveling*, hinged on two devices: **peripeteia** (a
reversal of the situation into its opposite) and **anagnorisis** (a recognition, a shift from
ignorance to knowledge). Freytag's later five-part arc — exposition, rising action, climax,
falling action, catastrophe — is one *tragic* specialization of this, not a universal template.

## Use when
Laying out act/sequence structure and stress-testing an event graph. Look for the reversal and
the recognition; a plot without either tends to be episodic ("and then… and then").

## Diagnostic questions
- What is the single action this whole piece completes? (Unity.)
- Where is the reversal — the point the situation turns to its opposite?
- Where is the recognition, and who does the recognizing (character, reader, both)?
- Remove any scene: does the through-action still complete? If yes, was it structural?

## Failure modes
- Episodic accumulation with no reversal (a list, not a plot).
- A "climax" that is only the loudest scene, not a turn.
- Freytag's pyramid applied mechanically to a quiet or non-tragic form.
- Recognition delivered as an info-dump rather than earned.

## Conflicts with
Non-linear discourse and open forms — structure is a fabula property; the telling may reorder or
withhold it ([[fabula-vs-discourse]]).
