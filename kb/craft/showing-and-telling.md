# Showing and Telling (Scene vs. Summary)

**Concept:** showing-and-telling · **Layer:** craft
**Used by:** `style-editor` agent, `narrative-architect` agent

## Definition
Two modes of rendering, distinguished mainly by *duration*. **Scene** (drama / "showing")
renders in near-real-time: action, dialogue, moment-to-moment perception. **Summary** (picture /
"telling") compresses time and reports. Lubbock's "picture vs. drama" and James's scenic method
name the same axis. The workshop slogan "show, don't tell" is a half-truth: telling is essential
for transition, time-compression, and voice. The craft is *choosing* the mode per beat, and
putting scene where the value changes.

## Use when
Deciding whether a beat should be dramatized or summarized; diagnosing prose that is flat
(over-summarized) or bloated (everything dramatized, including transitions).

## Diagnostic questions
- Does this moment change a value? Then it probably wants a scene.
- Is this a bridge in time or space? Then it probably wants summary — don't dramatize an errand.
- Am I "telling" an emotion a dramatized behavior could imply? (Cut the telling.)
- Am I dramatizing something the reader would rather I skip? (Summarize it.)

## Failure modes
- Everything summarized: a synopsis with no lived moments.
- Everything dramatized: real-time tedium, no compression, no pace.
- Told emotion standing in for shown behavior ([[concrete-vs-abstract]]).

## Conflicts with
Narrative distance — deep close third can "tell" interiority legitimately; the modes interact
([[narrative-distance]]).
