# Promise and Payoff

**Concept:** promise-and-payoff · **Layer:** craft
**Used by:** `src/fiction_compiler/hard_audit.py:audit_canon`, `continuity-auditor`

## Definition
A *promise* is an expectation the text deliberately raises in the reader — a planted question,
a loaded object, a foreshadowed threat, a withheld answer. A *payoff* discharges it. Promises
are debts: every one opened should be closed, subverted, or deliberately (visibly) refused.
Setups paid off create the retrospective click of "of course"; promises silently dropped
create the itch of a story that forgot itself.

## Use when
Opening any expectation, and at chapter/act boundaries. The canon audit tracks the ledger:
a delta may not close a promise never opened; promises still open at the end are reported so
you decide — pay off, subvert, or intentionally leave ambiguous.

## Diagnostic questions
- What question does this plant in the reader's mind? When will it be answered?
- Is every open promise on a schedule, or just floating?
- Is a payoff prepared *before* it lands (so it feels inevitable, not arbitrary)?

## Failure modes
- Dangling promise: a loaded detail that never returns (unless ambiguity is the contract).
- Unprepared payoff: a resolution with no earlier setup (feels like a cheat).
- Over-literal payoff: discharging a promise so tidily it loses resonance.

## Conflicts with
Ambiguity as an intended affect — some works keep promises deliberately open. Record that in
the reader contract so the audit's "open at end" note is read as intent, not defect.
