# Narrative Distance

**Concept:** narrative-distance · **Layer:** style
**Used by:** `style-editor` agent, `planning/style-profile.json`

## Definition
*Narrative (psychic) distance* is how near the prose sits to a character's consciousness — a
continuous dial from wide exterior report to deep interior. Roughly:
1. Distant report ("It was winter in the northern town.")
2. Exterior with orientation ("Jonas walked the harbor road.")
3. Close behavior/perception ("He counted the boats. Two were missing.")
4. Free indirect ("Two gone. Nobody had said a word to him about it.")
5. Interior monologue (near-direct thought).

Skilled prose *moves* along this dial deliberately; it does not sit at one setting by accident.
Free indirect style (level 4) fuses narrator and character so judgment and idiom blur — the
workhorse of modern close third.

## Use when
Setting a style profile's `narrative_distance` and diagnosing tonal flatness or unwanted
authorial intrusion.

## Diagnostic questions
- What distance is this passage at, and did I choose it?
- Do filter words ("she felt/saw/realized") pin me at level 3 when I want level 4?
- Does distance *shift* for effect (pull back for a turn, push in for a blow)?

## Failure modes
- Filtered perception where free indirect would be tighter (see [[concrete-vs-abstract]]).
- Distance whiplash with no purpose.
- Deep interiority that over-explains emotion the behavior already showed.

## Conflicts with
Focalization limits — deeper distance narrows to the focalizer's knowledge ([[focalization-and-knowledge]]).
