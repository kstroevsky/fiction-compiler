# The Static Scene (the lyric / observational mode)

**Concept:** static-scene · **Layer:** narratology
**Used by:** `adversarial-reader` agent, `narrative-architect` agent
**Evidence strength:** contested (a counter-tradition to the change-driven scene; mode-dependent)

## Claim
Not every compelling scene *turns a value*. A whole tradition — lyric, observational, ritual, and
much modernist fiction — builds scenes that hold still on the surface and derive their force from
**perceptual precision, voice, pattern, or slow transformation of attention**, not from a shift in
desire-versus-conflict. This card exists as the deliberate **counterweight** to `scene-dramaturgy`
and `eventfulness`, which are useful heuristics but become false when applied as universal law.

## Use when
A scene reads as "not turning" and a change-driven check ([[eventfulness]], [[scene-dramaturgy]])
wants to flag it inert. Before flagging, decide which mode the scene is in and what the reader
contract promised.

## The distinction that matters
A static scene is **not** the same as an inert scene:
- **Static and alive:** attention itself moves — a perception deepens, a pattern completes, an image
  accrues weight, a ritual is enacted with charged precision. Nothing "happens," yet the reader is
  changed.
- **Inert (a real defect):** motion without perceptual charge — an errand, a logistics beat, a
  canonical happening narrated at length. This is what `eventfulness` rightly catches.

The test is not "did a value turn?" but "did the reader's *perception* move, and did the contract
ask for that?"

## Diagnostic questions
- Is the force here voice, texture, pattern, or ritual — or is it just absence of event?
- Does the reader contract prize small things rendered precisely, or eventful drama?
- If I cut this scene, does a slow accumulation the ending needs collapse?

## Conflicts with
[[eventfulness]] and [[scene-dramaturgy]] — deliberately. Those cards maximize change; this one
insists change is one mode among several. Calibrate to the contract; do not let either win by default.

## Counterexample (guards against over-use)
A scene that claims the static mode but is merely an errand with no perceptual charge is **inert**,
and `eventfulness` is right to flag it. This card is not a license for flat writing.
